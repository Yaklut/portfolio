Bank Muamalat (BMI) Sales Performance Dashboard

Four relational tables joined into one BigQuery master table and a Looker Studio sales dashboard, with recommendations on product portfolio and customer retention.

Program
Project-Based Virtual Internship — Business Intelligence Analyst · Bank Muamalat × Rakamin Academy
Period
Oct 6–Nov 3, 2025
Data
4 relational tables · 3,339 orders · Jan 2020–Dec 2021
Tools
  • Google BigQuery
  • Google Looker Studio
  • SQL
01

Context & question

Bank Muamalat, Indonesia's first Sharia bank (founded 1991), needed a data-driven way to evaluate sales performance across products, customer segments and cities. The data arrived as four related tables that had to be understood and combined before any analysis was possible.

Data provided

  • Customer — contact details and city
  • Orders — date, customer, product and quantity (3,339 orders)
  • Product — name, category and price (70 products)
  • ProductCategory — category names (7 categories)
02

Approach

  1. Identify primary keys

    CustomerID, OrderID, ProdNumber and CategoryID — one per table.

  2. Map the relationships

    One customer has many orders, one product appears in many orders, and one category holds many products.

  3. Build the master table

    One BigQuery query joins the four tables and adds total_sales = quantity × price. The result has 3,339 rows — one per order — with no duplicated rows after the joins.

  4. Build the dashboard

    Looker Studio filters, scorecards, sales and quantity by product category, top-5 categories by sales and by quantity, sales by city and a monthly sales trend.

  5. Recommend

    Turned the patterns into recommendations for the business.

04

What the data shows

  • Revenue mix

    Two categories carry the revenue

    Robots and Drones bring in 69.6% of sales from only 19.6% of units, at average prices of about 706 and 389 per unit.

    Robots 42.4% + Drones 27.2% of sales

  • Volume vs value

    Volume and value point in opposite directions

    eBooks and Training Videos make up 44.7% of units but only 8.0% of sales — their average price is about 19 and 39 per unit.

    eBooks 26.8% + Training Videos 17.9% of units

  • Retention

    Repeat buying is already the norm

    The 3,339 orders came from 1,671 customers — two orders each on average — and 59.1% of customers ordered more than once.

    Up to 7 orders from one customer

  • Geography

    Sales are spread thin across cities

    Washington leads with 3.2% of sales (55,382); the top six cities together account for 11.5% across 361 cities.

    Washington · Houston · Sacramento · San Diego · Albany · Springfield

  • Trend

    Sales fluctuate rather than hold steady

    Monthly sales ranged from 52.3k (Oct 2021) to 95.4k (Jun 2021) around an average of 73.1k, and 2021 finished 7.8% below 2020.

    2020: 913.2k · 2021: 841.5k

Recommendations

  • From the original deck

    Position Robots and Drones as premium

    Position the two high-value categories as premium products, and pair them with Sharia financing options (Mudharabah/Murabahah with flexible tenors) to make higher ticket prices easier to buy.

    69.6% of sales from 19.6% of units

  • From the original deck

    Bundle the digital products

    Create bundles and subscription-based models for eBooks and Training Videos, where volume is high but each sale is small.

    44.7% of units · 8.0% of sales

  • From the original deck

    Strengthen retention with data

    Build a data-driven CRM with tiered loyalty rewards and targeted promotions based on transaction history — repeat buying is already common.

    59.1% of customers ordered more than once
