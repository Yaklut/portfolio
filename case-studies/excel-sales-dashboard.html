Sales & financial performance

Sales Performance Dashboard in Excel

Seven hundred raw orders turned into calculated revenue and profit, summarised in PivotTables and presented as an Excel dashboard with five charts and three slicers.

Program
MySkill Intensive Bootcamp — Microsoft Excel Basic to Advanced · Batch 36. The final project report was graded Distinction (total score 89).
Period
Sep 12–Sep 26, 2026 (final project report)
Data
700 orders · 6 products · 5 countries · 5 buyer segments · Sep 2013–Dec 2014
Tools
  • Microsoft Excel
  • XLOOKUP
  • PivotTables
  • Slicers
01

Context & question

The bootcamp's final-project brief supplied a raw order sheet and asked for a full analysis: build an order code, extract the order details into columns, calculate total price, revenue and gross profit, work out operating and net profit per year, and present the insights as a dashboard with at least four charts and one slicer. This project meets all of it, with five charts and three slicers.

Data provided

  • Order raw — 700 orders: country, buyer segment, product, quantity sold, discount, and a text field holding the order date
  • Product — manufacturing cost and selling price for 6 products
  • Discount — the definition of the Low, Medium and High discount tiers
  • Other financials — yearly G&A cost, handling cost and tax
02

Approach

  1. Prepare

    Built each order code from country and segment lookups plus the date, and pulled the day, month and year out of the order-details text to rebuild a real date column.

  2. Calculate

    Looked up each product's selling price and manufacturing cost, then computed Total Price, Revenue (after discount), Gross Profit and a discount tier for every order.

  3. Analyse

    Worked out yearly gross profit, operating profit (minus G&A and handling cost) and net profit (minus tax) from the Other financials sheet.

  4. Visualize

    Summarised the data in 12 PivotTables and built a dashboard sheet: four KPI cards, five charts, Top Product and Top Segment rankings, and slicers for order year, buyer segment and country.

Design decisions

  • Decision

    Discount tiers use rounded values

    Discount values carry tiny decimal noise (for example 0.0100013), so each tier is assigned from ROUND(discount × 100, 0) rather than the raw number.

  • Decision

    2013 is flagged as a partial year

    The data starts in September 2013, so 2013 has four months and 2014 has twelve. The product-growth chart says so in its subtitle, and the dashboard makes no year-over-year growth claim.

  • Decision

    Net profit is labelled “est.”

    Its cost lines (G&A, handling, tax) are yearly totals supplied in the brief, not per-order costs, so the dashboard card reads “Net Profit (est.)”.

The formulas

The main formulas behind the workbook, as written in the file. Ranges are trimmed slightly for readability.

Order code

="ORD-"&XLOOKUP(C2,Guide!$B$22:$B$26,Guide!$C$22:$C$26)
 &"-"&XLOOKUP(D2,Guide!$B$29:$B$33,Guide!$C$29:$C$33)
 &"-"&TEXT(H2,"yyyymmdd")          // e.g. ORD-GER-GOV-20141201

Extract the order date from the details text

// Order details is plain text, not valid JSON, so day/month/year are cut out of it
=DATE( VALUE(TEXTBEFORE(TEXTAFTER(B2,"""year"": """),"""")),
       VALUE(TEXTBEFORE(TEXTAFTER(B2,"""month"": """),"""")),
       VALUE(TEXTBEFORE(TEXTAFTER(B2,"""date"": """),"""")) )

Price, revenue, gross profit and discount tier

Selling Price  =XLOOKUP(E2,Product!$A$2:$A$7,Product!$C$2:$C$7)
Total Price    =I2*F2
Revenue        =J2*(1-IF(G2="-",0,G2))
Gross Profit   =K2-(XLOOKUP(E2,Product!$A$2:$A$7,Product!$B$2:$B$7)*F2)
Discount Tier  =IFS(G2=0,"No Discount", ROUND(G2*100,0)<=4,"Low",
                    ROUND(G2*100,0)<=9,"Medium", ROUND(G2*100,0)<=15,"High")

Revenue = total price − discount; gross profit = revenue − manufacturing cost × quantity.

Operating and net profit by year

// Other financials: yearly operating cost and net profit
Operating Profit (2013) =SUMPRODUCT((YEAR('Order raw'!H2:H701)=2013)*'Order raw'!L2:L701) - G&A - Handling
Net Profit      (2013)  =Operating Profit - Tax

Operating profit = gross profit − G&A − handling cost; net profit = operating profit − tax.

04

What the data shows

  • Concentration

    Two products carry the revenue

    Amarilla and VTT together bring in 71.7% of revenue; the other four products share the remaining 28.3%.

    $49.98M + $46.93M of $135.19M

  • Margin

    Revenue leader is not the profit leader

    VTT is 34.7% of revenue but earns only a 10.1% gross margin, against 37.9% for Amarilla. Amarilla alone produces about half of all gross profit.

    Gross profit: Amarilla $18.94M · VTT $4.73M

  • Discounts

    Deeper discounts, thinner margins

    Gross margin falls at every step: 34.3% with no discount, 30.7% (Low), 27.5% (Medium) and 25.3% (High). The Medium and High tiers still hold 70.9% of revenue.

    High tier: 245 orders · Medium: 242 · Low: 160 · None: 53

  • Segments

    Government is the largest buyer segment

    Government accounts for 42.1% of revenue from 300 of the 700 orders. The other four segments follow at 13.0–15.6% each. By country, revenue is fairly even (16.9–21.4%), with Mexico lowest.

    Government $56.85M · Channel Partners $21.08M · Small Business $19.91M

05

Challenges & takeaways

  1. 01

    The dates were buried in text

    Challenge

    Order details came as one text field. It looks like JSON but is not valid JSON, so a JSON parser could not read it.

    What I did

    Used TEXTAFTER and TEXTBEFORE to cut out the day, month and year, then rebuilt them with DATE so every order has a real date.

    Takeaway

    Look at how the text is actually structured before choosing a tool for it.

  2. 02

    Discounts that looked exact but were not

    Challenge

    Discount values carry tiny decimal noise (for example 0.0100013), so comparing them straight against the tier limits is unreliable.

    What I did

    Rounded discount × 100 to a whole percent before assigning the Low, Medium or High tier.

    Takeaway

    Round before comparing a value with a threshold.

  3. 03

    A year that is only a third of a year

    Challenge

    2013 has only September to December, so a plain 2013 versus 2014 comparison would make 2014 look like dramatic growth.

    What I did

    Flagged the partial year in the product-growth chart subtitle and on this page, and made no year-over-year growth claim.

    Takeaway

    Check how much of each period the data covers before comparing periods.
