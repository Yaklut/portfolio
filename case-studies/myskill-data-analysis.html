Context & question

The final project came as stakeholder emails, each with a deadline: a board report, a restock plan, a marketing question. The dataset is a fictional marketplace called Tokopaedi. The SQL track used six tables covering 2020 to 2024; the Python and dashboard tracks used one joined order table for 2021 to 2022.

Approach

  1. Read the brief

    Turned each email into one question, one table and one output, and wrote down the filter it implied (paid orders only, a calendar year, one event type).

  2. Query

    Wrote the five SQL answers in BigQuery and the four Python answers in Colab with pandas, after joining the order, product, payment and customer tables.

  3. Check

    Re-ran the totals a second way, looked for single orders that move a whole month, and checked how many days each month actually contains.

  4. Present

    Built the two-page Looker Studio dashboard and the slide decks, with each insight followed by one action the requester could take.

Design decisions

  • Decision

    Only paid orders count as sold

    Quantity, revenue and channel figures use is_valid = 1. The checkout-but-unpaid list is the exception, because that request asked for exactly those customers.

  • Decision

    Year filters use EXTRACT, not BETWEEN

    The date columns carry a time, so BETWEEN '2024-01-01' AND '2024-12-31' stops at midnight on 31 December. Filtering on the year keeps the last day.

  • Decision

    Net profit and AOV defined once

    Net profit = sales before discount − COGS × quantity. AOV = sales before discount ÷ unique orders. The dashboard, slides and Python use the same two formulas.

Selected code

Channel revenue, 2024 against the same month of 2023 (BigQuery)

SQLSQL
WITH monthly AS (
  SELECT channel_type,
         EXTRACT(YEAR  FROM order_date) AS yr,
         EXTRACT(MONTH FROM order_date) AS bulan,
         COUNT(DISTINCT order_id)       AS total_orders,
         SUM(after_discount)            AS revenue
  FROM `finpro-514510-t3.dataset.order_detail`
  WHERE is_valid = 1
    AND EXTRACT(YEAR FROM order_date) IN (2023, 2024)
  GROUP BY channel_type, yr, bulan
)
SELECT c.channel_type, c.bulan,
       c.total_orders AS orders_2024,
       c.revenue      AS revenue_2024,
       p.revenue      AS revenue_2023,
       ROUND(100 * SAFE_DIVIDE(c.revenue - p.revenue, p.revenue), 2) AS yoy_growth_pct
FROM monthly AS c
LEFT JOIN monthly AS p
  ON p.channel_type = c.channel_type AND p.bulan = c.bulan AND p.yr = 2023
WHERE c.yr = 2024
ORDER BY c.channel_type, c.bulan;

The brief said month-over-month, but it asked for the same month a year earlier, so the column is named for what it is: year-over-year.

# 1. Filter transaksi valid dan batasi Oktober - Desember 2022
df_valid = df[df['is_valid'] == 1].copy()
df_valid['order_date'] = pd.to_datetime(df_valid['order_date'])
q4 = df_valid[(df_valid['order_date'] >= '2022-10-01') & (df_valid['order_date'] <= '2022-12-31')].copy()

# 2. Nama bulan dan tipe hari (Sabtu=5, Minggu=6 -> Weekend)
q4['nama_bulan'] = q4['order_date'].dt.month_name()
q4['tipe_hari'] = q4['order_date'].dt.dayofweek.apply(lambda x: 'Weekend' if x >= 5 else 'Weekday')

# 3. Total penjualan per hari, lalu rata-rata harian per bulan dan tipe hari
harian = q4.groupby(['order_date', 'nama_bulan', 'tipe_hari'])['before_discount'].sum().reset_index()
rata_harian = (
    harian.groupby(['nama_bulan', 'tipe_hari'])['before_discount']
    .mean()
    .unstack()
    .reindex(['October', 'November', 'December'])
)

# 4. Selisih dan persentase perubahan weekend terhadap weekday
rata_harian['selisih'] = rata_harian['Weekend'] - rata_harian['Weekday']
rata_harian['peningkatan_persen'] = (rata_harian['selisih'] / rata_harian['Weekday'] * 100).round(2)
rata_harian['ada_peningkatan'] = rata_harian['selisih'] > 0
display(rata_harian)

What the data shows

  • Outlier

    Two orders made the best month look like the worst margin

    Two orders of 1,000 units each (6 August and 6 September 2022) are 20.2% of 2022 sales but only 6.3% of net profit. They pushed August and September average order value above Rp5.4M and margin down to about 11%.

    Without them: AOV Rp1.37M (Aug) and Rp1.28M (Sep), margin about 25%

  • Channels

    App Store lost value per order, not customers

    App Store revenue fell 18.1% from 2023 while its paid orders fell only 3.7%. Revenue per order dropped from Rp5.08M to Rp4.32M. Offline Store grew 77% on 12.7% more orders.

    App Store 1,138 → 1,096 orders · Rp5.78B → Rp4.73B

  • Campaign

    The weekend campaign did not lift weekend sales

    From October to December 2022, an average weekday sold Rp7.52M and an average weekend day Rp5.27M. Weekends were lower in every month, by 6.9% to 51.9%.

    Caveat: Q4 2022 data covers days 1–8 of each month only, so 7 weekend days in total

  • Funnel

    Organic conversion is the same on every channel

    Website brings the most organic traffic (29,062 events) and the lowest conversion, 2.16%, against 2.20% for App Store and 2.21% for Play Store. A 0.05-point gap is too small to rank the channels.

    83,944 organic events · 1,817 converted orders in 2024

  • Onboarding

    New customers wait over two months to buy

    The 187 customers who registered in 2024 took 67 to 78 days on average to place a first order (Website 66.9, Offline Store 75.0, Mobile App 78.2). New sign-ups also fell from 31 in January to 2 in December.

    Fastest first order: 2 days after registering

  • Payments

    Digital payments rarely complete

    In 2022, 86.3% of cash-on-delivery orders were paid, against 31.3% for Payaxis and 21.2% for Easypay. Only 353 of 2,359 customers (15.0%) ordered more than once.

    COD is 48.2% of units sold

05

Challenges & takeaways

  1. 01

    A date filter that dropped New Year's Eve

    Challenge

    Our first funnel query used BETWEEN '2024-01-01' AND '2024-12-31'. The column holds a time, so events after midnight on 31 December were left out: 28,976 Website events instead of 29,062.

    What I did

    Filtered on EXTRACT(YEAR FROM …) = 2024 in every query and re-ran the five SQL answers.

    Takeaway

    When a date column carries a time, filter by the period, not by two dates.

  2. 02

    A brief that said one thing and meant another

    Challenge

    The marketing email asked for month-over-month growth "against the same month in 2023". Those are two different measures.

    What I did

    Built the year-over-year version the example described, with a self-join on channel and month, and named the column yoy_growth_pct.

    Takeaway

    Name a metric for what it computes, and say so to the person who asked.

  3. 03

    One order that picks the prize

    Challenge

    The year-end prize list is the top 5 Mobiles & Tablets products by units. Number one, IDROID_BALRX7-Gold, sold 1,000 units in a single order; number two sold 31.

    What I did

    Kept the ranking as asked but stated in the answer that the top spot comes from one buyer, and separated both 1,000-unit orders in the dashboard insights.

    Takeaway

    Before a ranking turns into a decision, check how many orders sit behind the top entry.

  4. 04

    Months that are only a week long

    Challenge

    In the 2022 dashboard data, August to December contain 8 or 9 days each. Monthly order counts fall from about 390 to about 120 without any change in demand.

    What I did

    Compared orders per day (about 14 all year) instead of per month, and printed the caveat on the chart and in the weekend answer.

    Takeaway

    Count the days in each period before reading a drop as a decline.

    Discounts above 20% wiped out the profit

    For the data-visualisation task I built a two-page Looker Studio dashboard and a four-slide story on a Superstore dataset. Orders with discounts above 20% were 13.9% of transactions, and 96.8% of them lost money, a combined −$135.4K. Without them, margin would rise from 12.5% to 21.8%.

    Texas: $170K sales, −$25.7K profit, average discount 37%

    A cost gap broken down to one line

    For a ride-hailing case, quarterly cost had risen to IDR 5,050B against IDR 3,200B a year earlier while revenue halved. Infrastructure explained IDR 1,500B of the IDR 1,850B gap (81%), so the objective became infrastructure at or below IDR 2,500B by Q1 2024.

    Framework: clarify, break down, objective, root cause, countermeasures
