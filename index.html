Context

Kimia Farma, one of Indonesia's largest pharmaceutical companies, needed a data-driven way to evaluate business performance across its branch network.

My contribution

I imported and integrated four datasets — transactions, products, inventory, and branch offices — into Google BigQuery covering 2020–2023. I wrote SQL queries using CTEs, LEFT JOINs, and CASE WHEN logic to compute nett sales and gross profit margins by product price tier, then built a Looker Studio dashboard to surface the results.

Key insight

Jawa Barat led every province in both nett sales (Rp94.87B, 29.5% of the total) and transaction volume (198,723) — though nett sales held roughly flat year over year (~Rp80B annually, 2020–2023) rather than growing. Completed with an average score of 91.56 (Excellent).

How I work

The workflow behind the Kimia Farma analysis, from raw CSVs to a decision-ready dashboard.

  1. 01

    Load

    Imported four CSVs into BigQuery with explicit schemas. Dates arrive as M/D/YYYY text, so they were loaded as strings and parsed later.

  2. 02

    Model

    Built one analysis table with LEFT JOINs, then derived nett sales and nett profit using a price-tier margin rule.

  3. 03

    Validate

    Wrote checks for row counts, unparsed dates, unmatched joins and yearly totals, with expected values computed from the source CSVs.

  4. 04

    Report

    Connected the table to Looker Studio: filters, KPI tiles, a yearly trend, a province map, and a one-line takeaway at the top of the page.

Judgment calls I documented

  • Inventory is not joined to transactions. Its (branch, product) pairs repeat, so a direct join would duplicate sales rows and inflate nett sales.
  • Branches are identified by ID and city, not name. branch_name has only three unique values.
  • Province ISO codes were added so the geo map places every province correctly.
